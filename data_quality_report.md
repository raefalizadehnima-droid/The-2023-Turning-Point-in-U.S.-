# Data-quality report

Generated by `ingest.qc_checks` and `harmonize.py`. Raw files were never modified.

## Key findings

- All three workbooks are **aggregate** (no individual-level records). SUDORS: jurisdiction-year with monthly counts; DOSE-SYS: jurisdiction-month ratios per 10,000 ED visits (no visit counts); DOSE-dx: jurisdiction-month counts and crude rates.
- SUDORS composite 'Overall' rows change membership by year (e.g. 42 jurisdictions in 2023 and 43 in 2024) and are **not comparable across years**; a fixed cohort of 27 jurisdictions was used. Recomputed cohort totals equal the published trend-tab totals.
- SUDORS sentinel 9999 appears in 38,084 cells (none in monthly death counts) and was treated as missing. Naive summation of such cells produces implausible totals.
- Each pairwise combination is stored twice in SUDORS; 139 combination variables reduce to 81 after name-based de-duplication (value equality alone would wrongly merge all-9999 columns).
- Published 'detected drug' percentages use a toxicology-known denominator absent from the file; own shares (deaths / all drug deaths) are conservative.
- DOSE-SYS: no values for March-August 2020 (excluded by CDC); fentanyl, heroin, cocaine, methamphetamine and benzodiazepine indicators are mostly suppressed or unreported; only all-drug, opioid and stimulant indicators have usable cohorts.
- DOSE-dx has no data dictionary; the meaning of empty (NaN) count cells is undocumented and they were treated as unavailable, never as zero.
- No preliminary-versus-final vintages are available; reporting delay therefore cannot be measured directly.
